The upper cervical handout describes a tool positioned near the junction between the skull and upper neck. Its sequence illustrates a glide in the direction opposite to a head turn and includes several body positions. The cervical lateral handout instead sets out side bending and rotation in separate sections.

Those differences are useful when looking up a handout or checking which material Scott discussed. The upper cervical protocol’s tool placement and combined movements make it different from an ordinary side tilt or turn. Its proposed mechanism is Scott’s explanation. The FAQ does not convert it into instructions for performing a tool-assisted technique on your own.
