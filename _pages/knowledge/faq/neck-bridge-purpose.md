In its written introduction and video, Scott presents the Neck Bridge as a tool he developed for work around the upper cervical spine. He discusses proprioception, the sense of position and movement, to explain his interest in that region. This is his rationale for developing the tool. Claims about broader changes in pain or movement reflect his interpretation and experience.

The neck flosser is a separate tool. Its basic demonstration focuses on tissues near the upper shoulder, and an advanced clip uses it during opposing neck and shoulder movement. The two names therefore refer to different demonstrations with different contact areas and aims. See the Neck Bridge video and its accompanying directions for details.

In his introduction Scott describes interest in the top one or two cervical segments. His emphasis is the positioning feedback he associates with that area. The flosser demonstration instead starts by identifying the upper shoulder muscles, so the clips answer different questions about the tools.
