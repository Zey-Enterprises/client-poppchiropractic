Someone may feel less pain while still finding a familiar activity difficult. The spinal guides distinguish the intensity of symptoms from functions such as moving the hips and trunk or returning to normal activities. The two kinds of information help explain why Scott asks about both how something feels and what someone is able to do.

The guides use the example of reduced hip motion during a back problem. That example illustrates that a movement can remain difficult while symptoms change. The original guide’s broader claims about permanent compensations are Scott’s clinical interpretation and are not a prognosis for an individual.
