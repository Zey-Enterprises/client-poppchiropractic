The Expansion Method draws on the development of movement, from an infant lengthening and rolling to sitting, standing and walking. It describes movement as alternating between opening and controlled contraction.

Treatment uses this model to set three goals: lengthening against gravity, rotating outward around the body’s vertical axis and returning from that lengthened position with a controlled contraction.

The movement examples progress from the infant’s head, arms and legs beginning to work with the trunk to the opposing motions Scott describes in crawling and walking. Sitting and standing introduce another problem in the same model: staying upright against gravity. These examples help explain why treatment can involve working on rotation while maintaining an upright or lengthened position.
