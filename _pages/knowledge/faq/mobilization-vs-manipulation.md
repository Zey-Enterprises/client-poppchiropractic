**Low-velocity mobilization** uses gentle stretching without a quick thrust. We can perform it in the office and teach some techniques for self-treatment.

**High-velocity manipulation** uses a quick thrust near the end of a joint’s range. It can make a popping sound. The techniques differ in how the movement is applied and in their risks and benefits.

The distinction is also about what can be taught outside the office. Some gentle mobilization movements can be taught for use between visits. Manipulation involves a quick thrust applied by the clinician. Hearing a pop is therefore not the definition of mobilization or the measure Scott gives for whether an exercise was done properly.
