**Cancer-specific mortality** counts deaths attributed to the cancer being studied. **All-cause mortality** counts deaths from any cause. A study can report a difference for one measure and find that the other estimate remains uncertain. The meta-analysis Scott cites reports the measures separately and gives uncertainty ranges for its estimated lifetime gains.{% include citation.html label="1" %}

These are outcomes measured across study groups. Use the distinction to interpret the article’s tables. Discuss individual screening decisions with the clinician responsible for your screening care.

The size of the study group, the length of follow-up and the range around an estimate also matter. An estimate close to zero with a wide range leaves more uncertainty than an equally small estimate with a narrow range. Neither number by itself captures every outcome that a person may weigh when making a screening decision.
