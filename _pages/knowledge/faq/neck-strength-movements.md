The video distinguishes three movements: retraction draws the head backward, flexion bends the neck, and rotation turns it. The demonstration points out where the sternocleidomastoid is and how it engages during active movement.

That is a different focus from the neck flosser clips, which demonstrate stretching with a tool near the upper shoulder. If you are looking for the handout on lateral neck movement, the cervical lateral exercises are a separate resource with their own setup and illustrations. Scott also attributes certain headaches to the muscle in the video. The demonstration does not establish the cause of a particular headache or which exercise is appropriate.

The names clarify why movement in this clip is not simply bending to one side. He discusses turning and moving the neck backward as distinct actions before focusing on a muscle. The printable lateral exercise is useful for identifying a different direction of movement in his library.
