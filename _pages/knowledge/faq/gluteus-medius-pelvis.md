During the single-leg portion of a walking step, the pelvis needs to remain controlled while the hip can still rotate. The video uses a pelvic lift, or hip hike, to show how the gluteus medius fits into both tasks.

The lift is demonstrated from three starting orientations: turned inward, neutral and turned outward. The movement remains vertical as the orientation changes. It does not determine which position an individual should train. The separate hip exercise material supplies more context for how Scott organizes rotational movement around the hip.

The main comparison is the same pelvic action with a different orientation of the body each time. When Scott talks about keeping the pelvis level in gait, he is explaining why he brings that controlled action into the exercise instead of only discussing the muscle in isolation.
