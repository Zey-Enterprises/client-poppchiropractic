The Expansion Method draws on developmental movements such as rolling, sitting and standing. In the standing example, the task is getting upright and maintaining that position against gravity. The walking example adds changes in direction and opposing rotations between body segments. The two examples help explain why Scott uses the word expansion for more than one kind of movement.

The method page relates that model to treatment goals such as lengthening, outward rotation and controlling the return. Those are goals within Scott’s clinical model. Statements about reflexes or the duration of treatment effects are proposals in that material, not guarantees about what happens after a particular visit.
