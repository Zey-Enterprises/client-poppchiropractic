## Manipulation and mobilization

We use two approaches to move a joint or surrounding tissue:

- **Low-velocity mobilization** uses gentle stretching without a quick thrust or the familiar joint pop. We can perform it in the office and teach you some techniques for use between visits.
- **High-velocity manipulation** uses a quick thrust near the end of a joint’s range of motion. It can produce a popping sound.

The goals include improving range of motion and tissue movement, easing spasm and stiffness, and supporting joint function. Each technique has different risks and benefits.

## Therapeutic exercise

Exercises may include resisted movement or stretching of individual spinal segments, balance work on a gym ball or wobble board, and general strength exercises such as deadlifts or overhead squats. We also teach postural and yoga exercises, breathing exercises, and ways to adjust how you sit or move during the day. Exercise selection depends on the goals of treatment.

![Illustration accompanying the therapeutic exercise description](/assets/images/legacy/76297ffbe2bcacd7cce8.jpg)

## Patient education and self-treatment

We teach techniques you can use between visits to take an active role in your recovery. The goal is to help you manage your condition more independently and use only as much in-office care as you need.
